# Evidence Integrity and Methodology

This document outlines the technical and procedural measures implemented to ensure evidence integrity, chain of custody, and the proper role of automation in the Title IX Victim Advocacy Platform.

## Source Retention and Hashing

All original source materials are retained in their unmodified form within the system database. This ensures that:

- **Original data preservation**: Source documents, emails, and communications are stored exactly as received, with no alterations to content or metadata.
- **Cryptographic verification**: Each source document is assigned a cryptographic hash (SHA-256) upon ingestion. This hash serves as a digital fingerprint that uniquely identifies the document and its exact contents.
- **Integrity validation**: At any point in the analytical process, the hash can be recomputed against the stored source to verify that the original document has not been modified, corrupted, or tampered with.
- **Audit trail**: Hash values are logged with timestamps and associated with the user, system process, or external source that introduced the document.

Cryptographic hashing verifies that the retained file matches the version recorded in the ingestion log. Combined with the immutable ingestion log timestamp, this provides assurance that the document has not been modified since recording.

## Provenance and Version Control

Full provenance tracking is maintained throughout the analytical lifecycle:

- **Source attribution**: Every document and derivative analytical artifact is tagged with its original source, collection method, and date of introduction into the system.
- **Chain of custody**: The system records which users, processes, or integrations have accessed, modified, or referenced each piece of evidence.
- **Version history**: All modifications to analytical summaries, categorizations, and notes are timestamped and reversible. No analytical conclusions are overwritten; all intermediate states are preserved.
- **Git integration**: System-generated analytical artifacts and policy configurations are version-controlled via Git, ensuring that changes to analytical frameworks and methodologies can be audited and reverted if necessary.
- **Metadata retention**: Document metadata (sender, recipient, timestamp, attachment information, etc.) is preserved separately and linked to the source hash, ensuring that metadata modifications do not affect the integrity verification of the underlying content.

This approach ensures that any party can trace the complete history of how evidence was collected, processed, and analyzed.

## Deterministic Packet Generation

Analytical outputs are generated using versioned, reproducible processes to support auditability:

- **Versioned transformations**: Analytical algorithms (keyword matching, entity extraction, relationship mapping) are version-controlled. Each analytical run records which algorithm version was applied, enabling reconstruction using archived versions.
- **Algorithm stability**: System-controlled algorithms are updated only through documented changes and are reversible. Previous analytical runs can be replicated by applying the same archived algorithm version to the source document.
- **Third-party models and embeddings**: Embedding generation and other calls to third-party models or machine learning runtimes depend on external service versions, API parameters, and runtime environments. These dependencies are recorded (model name, version, parameters), but exact reproducibility cannot be guaranteed across different runtime versions or service updates. Reproducibility is supported "where the underlying runtime permits."
- **Output validation**: Generated analytical packets document the processing pipeline, algorithm versions, and external dependencies used. This enables verification of what processing occurred but does not guarantee bit-for-bit reproducibility in all cases.

This ensures that analytical processing is transparent, auditable, and reproducible within the constraints of external dependencies.

## Human Review of All Conclusions

Automation is a tool for evidence organization and analysis assistance—not a substitute for human judgment or decision-making:

- **No automated conclusions**: The system does not render any legal findings, determinations of misconduct, or conclusions of liability. All such determinations are made exclusively by human reviewers.
- **Human review gates**: All analytical outputs generated by the system (summaries, categorizations, relationship mappings, keyword extractions) are marked as preliminary. Sean performs the final human review of analytical conclusions for this platform. Additional review by counsel, investigators, or subject matter experts occurs as specifically documented in individual cases.
- **Final determination**: Sean applies judgment about relevance, reliability, and materiality to system-generated insights. Any specialized review (legal counsel, investigator expertise, etc.) is identified and documented when it occurs.
- **Documented reasoning**: All human conclusions are documented with explicit reasoning that explains why particular evidence was considered, how it was weighted, and what determinations were reached. This documentation is retained as part of the analytical record.
- **Dispute resolution**: If there is disagreement between system-generated analysis and human judgment, human judgment prevails. The rationale for the disagreement is documented.

The system is designed to augment human decision-making by organizing evidence, identifying patterns, and flagging potential connections—not to replace it.

## Privacy Controls

Privacy-sensitive information is protected throughout the analytical process according to implementation:

- **Data classification**: Information is classified according to sensitivity levels (public, internal, sensitive, confidential). Privacy-sensitive data includes personally identifiable information (PII), health information, sexual assault trauma details, and any information that could identify or harm a witness or victim.
- **Access controls**: Access to evidence repositories is restricted to authorized personnel. The extent of role-based access controls (RBAC) is documented in the system's access control configuration.
- **De-identification for analysis**: Where possible, preliminary analytical processing (entity extraction, keyword matching, relationship mapping) is performed on de-identified versions of documents to reduce unnecessary exposure of PII to automated systems.
- **Audit of access**: Access to evidence, including by human users and automated processes, is logged for audit purposes.
- **Retention and deletion**: Information is retained according to institutional policies and legal requirements. Deletion is subject to applicable holds and administrative approval.

## Automation Assists Analysis; Does Not Make Legal Determinations

The distinction between analytical assistance and legal determination is fundamental to the system's design:

- **Analytical assistance**: The system can extract keywords, identify patterns, map relationships between entities, flag potential connections, and organize evidence chronologically. These are tools to support human analysis.
- **No legal authority**: The system has no authority to interpret law, apply legal standards, determine whether conduct violates policy, or decide outcomes of misconduct investigations. These determinations are exclusively within the domain of human decision-makers.
- **Transparency about limitations**: Users are informed about the capabilities and limitations of automated analysis. System-generated insights are clearly marked as preliminary, subject to human review, and not actionable without human validation.
- **Clear labeling**: All analytical outputs are labeled to indicate whether they represent preliminary system-generated analysis or final human conclusions.
- **Regulatory compliance**: The system is designed to comply with applicable regulations and institutional policies governing evidence handling, evidence-based decision-making, and due process.

## Conclusion

The Title IX Victim Advocacy Platform is built on the principle that technology can enhance evidence integrity, organization, and analysis while preserving human oversight and decision-making authority. Rigorous cryptographic verification, full provenance tracking, deterministic processing, mandatory human review, and robust privacy controls ensure that evidence is handled with appropriate care and that all conclusions rest on sound human judgment, not automation.
